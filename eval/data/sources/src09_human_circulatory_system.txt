The human cardiovascular system is a closed dual-circuit transport system consisting of the heart, blood vessels, and blood. Its fundamental physiological functions include delivering oxygen, nutrients, and hormones to peripheral tissues, while removing metabolic waste products such as carbon dioxide and nitrogenous compounds.

The heart is a four-chambered muscular pump located in the mediastinum. The right and left atria act as receiving chambers, while the right and left ventricles serve as pumping chambers. Deoxygenated blood returns from systemic tissues via the superior and inferior venae cavae into the right atrium. It flows through the tricuspid valve into the right ventricle, which pumps it through the pulmonary semilunar valve into the pulmonary trunk. In the pulmonary circuit, blood flows through pulmonary capillaries surrounding the alveoli of the lungs, where carbon dioxide diffuses out and oxygen binds to hemoglobin inside red blood cells (erythrocytes).

Oxygenated blood returns to the heart via four pulmonary veins entering the left atrium. It passes through the bicuspid (mitral) valve into the thick, highly muscular left ventricle. The left ventricle contracts with substantial pressure to propel oxygenated blood through the aortic semilunar valve into the systemic aorta, initiating systemic circulation to the remainder of the body.

The mechanical events of a single heartbeat comprise the cardiac cycle, subdivided into systole (myocardial contraction and blood ejection) and diastole (myocardial relaxation and chamber filling). Intrinsic electrical conduction coordinates this pumping action without requiring direct neural input. The sinoatrial (SA) node, situated in the superior wall of the right atrium, functions as the primary natural pacemaker, firing action potentials at approximately 60 to 100 beats per minute. Electrical impulses travel through atrial muscle to the atrioventricular (AV) node, undergo a brief delay allowing complete atrial emptying, and descend rapidly through the bundle of His, right and left bundle branches, and Purkinje fibers to trigger coordinated ventricular contraction from the apex upward.
