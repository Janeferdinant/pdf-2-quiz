Chemical thermodynamics governs the energy transformations and spontaneity of chemical and physical processes. The First Law of Thermodynamics, also called the law of conservation of energy, states that the total energy of an isolated system is constant; energy cannot be created or destroyed, only transformed from one form to another. Mathematically, the change in internal energy (Delta U) is given by Delta U = q + w, where q is heat absorbed by the system and w is work performed on the system.

Enthalpy (H) is defined as H = U + PV, where P is pressure and V is volume. In constant-pressure processes, the change in enthalpy (Delta H) equals the heat gained or lost by the system. Reactions that release heat to their surroundings have a negative Delta H and are termed exothermic, whereas reactions that absorb heat have a positive Delta H and are termed endothermic. Standard enthalpies of formation (Delta H_f_deg) are tabulated relative to pure elements in their most stable reference states at 1 bar and 298.15 K, which are defined as having an enthalpy of formation of zero.

The Second Law of Thermodynamics introduces entropy (S), a state function measuring the dispersal of energy or molecular disorder in a system. The second law states that in any spontaneous process, the total entropy of the universe (system plus surroundings) must increase: Delta S_universe = Delta S_system + Delta S_surroundings > 0. For a reversible process at equilibrium, Delta S_universe equals zero.

To determine whether a process will occur spontaneously at constant temperature and pressure without calculating entropy changes in the surroundings, chemists utilize Gibbs Free Energy (G), defined as G = H - TS. The change in Gibbs free energy for a process at constant temperature is Delta G = Delta H - T(Delta S). When Delta G is negative, the forward process is thermodynamically favorable (spontaneous). When Delta G is positive, the forward process is non-spontaneous, meaning the reverse process is spontaneous. When Delta G equals zero, the system is in dynamic equilibrium. A reaction that is both exothermic (Delta H < 0) and increases entropy (Delta S > 0) is spontaneous at all temperatures.
